Fitting and Thick Substrates

Two features aimed at modelling measured spectra: automatic differentiation, for gradient-based fits of thicknesses and indices, and incoherent (thick) layers, for substrates and windows whose interference fringes the spectrometer does not resolve.

Gradients with ForwardDiff

transfer and amplitude_coefficients are written generically in their number type, so ForwardDiff dual numbers pass through layer thicknesses, dispersion functions, Euler angles, permeabilities, sheet conductivities, λ, θ and μ. The gradient of a transmittance with respect to the film thicknesses:

using TransferMatrix, ForwardDiff

λ = 1e4 / 2100                                   # 2100 cm⁻¹, in μm
stack(p) = [Layer(λ -> 1.0, 0.0),                # ambient
            Layer(λ -> 2.4, p[1]),               # film 1, thickness p[1]
            Layer(λ -> 1.35 + p[3]*im, p[2]),    # film 2, thickness p[2], extinction p[3]
            Layer(λ -> 1.45, 0.0)]               # substrate

ForwardDiff.gradient(p -> transfer(λ, stack(p); θ=0.3).Tpp, [0.5, 0.9, 0.01])

The test suite checks these gradients against central finite differences (agreement better than 1e-6) on the 2×2 (:auto, :decoupled) and 4×4 (:exp) paths, for isotropic, anisotropic, rotated and gyrotropic layers, sheets, the circular basis and incoherent layers.

Not differentiable: method=:eig and an anisotropic or magnetic ambient or substrate whose own parameters (or θ) carry dual numbers, because both diagonalize with eigen. The sweeps and the field and energy functions return Float64.

Incoherent layers

A 3 mm CaF₂ window has etalon fringes about 1 cm⁻¹ apart, below the resolution of a typical FTIR measurement, so the instrument records their average. Mark such layers with incoherent:

air  = Layer(λ -> 1.0, 0.0)
caf2 = Layer(λ -> 1.40 + 1e-6im, 3000.0)         # 3 mm, in μm
H, L = Layer(λ -> 2.4, 0.5), Layer(λ -> 1.35, 0.9)

coated = [air, H, L, caf2, L, H, air]             # coatings on both faces
transfer(λ, coated; θ=0.2, incoherent=4)          # layer 4 is added by intensity

The stack is split at each incoherent layer into coherent blocks. Each block is solved with the 2×2 path for its forward and backward power reflectance and transmittance, and the blocks are chained through the incoherent layers' single-pass attenuation with the intensity-matrix method of Katsidis and Siapkas (Appl. Opt. 41, 3978, 2002). For a lossless thick layer the result equals the coherent spectrum averaged over one period of the layer's round-trip phase; the test suite checks this to 1e-12.

Several layers may be incoherent (incoherent=[3, 7]), and the keyword is also accepted by sweep_angle and sweep_thickness. Incoherent layers must be isotropic and non-magnetic, every coherent block must decouple s and p, and only basis=:linear is available; the cross-polarized terms are zero.
